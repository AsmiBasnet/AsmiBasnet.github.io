Case Study

Nepal Disaster Watch: turning scattered disaster evidence into one view that both a responder and an AI agent can use.

The problem

After floods and landslides cut off villages in Nepal, the evidence a responder needs is spread across separate sources: NDRRMA/BIPAD incidents, DHM river gauges, Department of Roads closures, UN GDACS alerts, Copernicus GloFAS forecasts and Copernicus EMS damage grading. A responder has to check each one separately.

What I built

A live Leaflet map app with 11 native WebMCP tools, built with Sushrit Kafle for OpenAI's WebMCP Challenge.

  • 6 read tools, including cross_reference_district, which compares evidence from every source across all 77 districts.
  • 5 write tools that pan the map, filter records and open drill-downs in sync with what the human is looking at.

What it measures and shows

11WebMCP tools (6 read, 5 write)
6institutional data sources
77districts cross-referenced
3feed states: live, unreachable, snapshot

Every feed reports its own status, so a source that is down shows up as unreachable instead of failing silently. That matters when someone is making decisions from the screen.

Honest limits

This is an independent, open-source project and not an official warning service. I am not quoting accuracy or speed numbers here. The counts above describe what it does.
